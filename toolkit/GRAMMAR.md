## 1. Sounds & pronunciation (Peace Corps L1, after Matumo 1993)

- Vowels: a (father), e (they/begin), i (deep), o (boat/put), u (rude). e and o each cover two sounds.
- `g` = guttural [x] (as in Afrikaans "g"); `r` rolled; `tl`/`tlh` lateral affricate ("cluck"-like), tlh aspirated
- Aspirated pairs matter: p/ph, t/th, k/kh, ts/tsh — aspiration changes meaning
- `tsh` like ts in "mats" (aspirated); `ch` as in church (loanwords)
- q, v, z essentially non-existent (loanwords only); x = click (rare, place names)
- **Tonal language**: high/low tone distinguishes homographs (see minimal pairs, Peace Corps L19):
  mabele (sorghum vs breasts), lapa (be tired vs home), goga (pull vs smoke),
  utlwa (feel/hear/taste), nna (me vs sit), fitlha (bury vs hide), di kae? (how many vs where)

## 2. Noun classes (SECL121 Unit 1 core)

Setswana nouns fall into classes marked by prefix; everything else in the sentence agrees
with the noun's class via concords. Standard Bantu numbering:

| Class | Prefix | Typical meaning | Example | Subject concord |
|-------|--------|-----------------|---------|-----------------|
| 1  | mo-  | person (sg)         | motho (person), morutabana (teacher) | o / a |
| 1a | ∅    | kinship/names       | rre, mme, malome | o / a |
| 2  | ba-  | people (pl)         | batho, barutabana | ba |
| 2a | bo-  | (pl of 1a)          | borre, bomme | ba |
| 3  | mo-  | non-person sg       | motse (village), mosese (dress) | o |
| 4  | me-  | (pl of 3)           | metse, mesese | e |
| 5  | le-  | various sg          | lebati (door), leina (name) | le |
| 6  | ma-  | (pl of 5)           | mabati, maina | a |
| 7  | se-  | things/languages    | setilo (chair), Setswana | se |
| 8  | di-  | (pl of 7)           | ditilo | di |
| 9  | n-/∅ | animals, loans (sg) | ntlo (house), pitse (horse), kofi | e |
| 10 | din- | (pl of 9)           | dintlo, dipitse | di |
| 11 | lo-  | long/thin things    | lonao (foot), losea (infant) | lo |
| 14 | bo-  | abstracts           | bogobe (porridge), botshelo (life) | bo |
| 15 | go-  | infinitives/gerunds | go ja (to eat / eating) | go |
| 16–18 | fa-/go-/mo- | locatives | fa, go, mo | go |

Memory hooks: **mo-/ba- = people**, **se-/di- = things (se- also = language/culture: Setswana!)**,
**le-/ma- = pairs & misc**, **9/10 = animals + borrowed words**, **bo- = abstract ideas**,
**go + verb = "to VERB"**.

## 3. Personal pronouns & subject concords (Peace Corps L8, L10, L11)

| Person | Pronoun (emphatic) | Subject concord |
|--------|--------------------|-----------------|
| I      | nna  | ke |
| you sg | wena | o  |
| he/she | ene  | o (present) / a (some tenses & negatives) |
| we     | rona | re |
| you pl | lona | le / lo |
| they   | bone | ba |

Emphatic pronouns are optional add-ons; the concord is obligatory:
*(Nna) ke a bereka* — "(Me,) I work."
**re = we** — hence the app name: Re:Lefela ≈ "we're (at) zero".

## 4. Tenses & negation — the master table (Peace Corps L10, verbatim source)

Model verb: **batla** (want)

| | Affirmative | Negative |
|---|---|---|
| Present | ke **a** batla | **ga** ke batl**e** |
| Past | ke **ne ke** batla | ke ne ke **sa** batl**e** |
| Future | ke **tlaa** batla (also written *tla*) | ga ke **kake ka** batla |

Rules (from source):
1. Negative present: prefix **ga** + final verb vowel -a → **-e** (ga ke batle, ga a je, ga ke rate kofi)
2. Negative past: **ne** + **sa** + -e ending (ke ne ke sa batle)
3. Negative future: **ga** + concord + **kake** + concord + verb (ga ke kake ka batla)
4. Present affirmative "a": appears when no object follows (ke **a** ja = I'm eating) and drops
   before objects (ke ja borotho = I eat bread) — long vs short present.
5. 3rd person sg concord shifts o → a in past/negative (o ne **a** batla, ga **a** batle)

Past-tense verb forms are irregular-ish (perfect stem -ile and friends):
batla→batlile, ja→jele, nwa→nole, tsamaya→tsamaile, tla→tlile, reka→rekile, bona→bonye,
tsaya→tsere, robala→robetse … full list per verb in sentence-bank / Peace Corps L5.

## 5. "To be" and "to have" (Peace Corps L11; copulatives = SECL121 Unit 2)

- Progressive "be doing": concord + **a** + verb — *ke a bala* (I am reading)
- **To have = na le**: *ke na le buka* (I have a book); negative: *ga ke na buka* / *ga ke na le madi*
- Identifying copulative **ke**: *leina lame ke Itumeleng* (my name is Itumeleng); *ke morutabana* (I am a teacher)
- Descriptive/locative: *o kae?* (where is he/she?), *o teng* (he/she is present/fine),
  *go siame* (it's fine/OK)
- Weather/impersonal **go**: *go serame* (it's cold), *go mogote* (it's hot), *go letsatsi* (sunny)

## 6. Question words (Peace Corps L12; SECL121 Unit 2)

eng? what — *ke eng?* | leng? when — *o tsile leng?* | kae? where — *o tswa kae?*
jang? how — *o tsogile jang?* | mang? who — *o mang?* | efe/dife? which — *o batla efe?*
(ka) go reng? why | bokae? how much — *ke bokae?* | a …? yes/no marker — *a o tswa kwa Amerika?*

Yes = **ee**; No = **nnyaa**.

## 7. Connectors (Peace Corps L9)

le (and, with) · mme (but) · kgotsa / kana (or) · jaaka (as/like) · ka jalo (therefore) · ka gore (because)

## 8. Greetings pack (SECL121 Unit 1 heart; Peace Corps L2–L4)

- Dumela rra / mma — hello sir / madam; plural: **Dumelang** borra le bomma
- O tsogile jang? — how are you? (lit. how have you risen) → Ke tsogile sentle
- Le kae? / O kae? — how are you (informal, lit. where are you) → Re teng / Ke teng
- Re a leboga — thank you (we thank); Ke itumetse — thank you (I am glad); ke a leboga — I thank you
- Go siame — goodbye/OK; Sala sentle — stay well (to one staying); Tsamaya sentle — go well
- Tlhola sentle — have a good day; Robala sentle / boroko — good night
- Ke tla go bona (kamoso / kgantele) — see you (tomorrow / later)
- Intshwarele — excuse me / forgive me; Tsweetswee — please
